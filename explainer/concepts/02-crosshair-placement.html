02 · CROSSHAIR PLACEMENT & SETUP COST

Why does pre-aiming save lives?

Turning your crosshair is not instant. The angular distance between where you are looking and where the enemy appears is called Setup Cost (q). Pre-aiming removes physical aiming travel before the fight begins.

✗ LAZY CROSSHAIR (AIMING AT WALL)
Total Time: 404 ms (Reaction + Slew + Fire) Deadline: 350 ms
A = 165ms
q = 139ms
p = 100ms
DEADLINE
⚠️ DEADLINE BREACH (+54ms Too Slow)

Because you were looking at the wall, you had to react (165ms), rotate your mouse 53° (139ms), and fire (100ms). The enemy fired at 350ms before your reticle finished traveling.

✓ PRE-AIMING THE CORNER (ZERO SETUP COST)
Total Time: 265 ms (Reaction + Fire) Margin: +85 ms Survived
A = 165ms
p = 100ms
DEADLINE (350ms)
✓ TARGET ELIMINATED (+85ms Tactical Margin)

Because your crosshair was already resting on the corner edge, slew time was 0 ms. You reacted and fired in 265 ms—beating the enemy's 350 ms trigger by 85 ms.

📐 Mathematical Definition: Setup Cost (q) in the Scheduling Compiler
q = Δθ / ωaim

In the scheduling compiler, every threat job Jj requires a release time rj, sensorimotor reaction latency Aplayer, setup cost qj, and service duration pj:

Completion Time Cj = rj + Aplayer + ( |θcurrent − θtarget| / ωaim ) + pweapon

When you maintain proper crosshair placement, |θcurrent − θtarget| ≈ 0, which reduces qj → 0. This saves up to 150–250 ms—equivalent to a professional esports player's entire reaction time—purely through spatial discipline rather than raw genetics.
