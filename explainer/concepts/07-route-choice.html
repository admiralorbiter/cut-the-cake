07 · TRAJECTORY CONDITIONING & THE SLEW THRASH PARADOX

Can the exact same room have two different answers?

Yes. The physical path you choose dictates the sequence and angular geometry of threat reveals. Taking the shorter corner-cut shortcut can cause an alternating 240° wrist-thrash trap, while a wide outer arc yields a smooth 60° sweep.

🔄 THE PHYSICAL PHENOMENON: MONOTONIC SWEEP VS. ZIGZAG WRIST THRASH
❌ The Corner-Cut Shortcut (Thrash Trap)
Cutting tight around the central pillar reveals threats in an alternating zigzag: Target 1 (up-right), Target 3 (down-right), Target 2 (up-right). The player's wrist must thrash back and forth across 240° of rotation, blowing the reaction deadline.
✓ The Wide Outer Arc (Smooth Sweep)
Walking the outer perimeter unrolls the encounters monotonically from left to right (0° → 30° → 60°). Total rotation is only 60°, clearing all 3 threats well inside safety margins.

Why Static Heatmaps Fail: Spatial Memory in Motion

Traditional level design analysis uses static 2D threat heatmaps. But heatmaps treat player state as memoryless. In real gameplay, your reticle has spatial state (current heading θ):

1. Path Defines the Job Sequence

Changing trajectory γ shifts the reveal timestamps rj(γ) and relative bearings θj(γ). A room is never inherently safe or dangerous on its own—it is safe or dangerous relative to a specific traversal route.

2. Slew Thrash Overhead

When targets appear alternately on opposite sides of your motion vector, the reticle incurs quadratic switching penalties (Δθ12 + Δθ23 + &dots;). The optimal route minimizes total directional reversals.

Tactical clearability belongs to a trajectory through space—not the static room in isolation.
