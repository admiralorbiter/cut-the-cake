04 · TARGET CLEARANCE ORDER

Why does the order you clear angles matter?

The enemies in the room do not change. But your clearance sequence dictates how much turning and waiting gets inserted between each shot. Even shooting the center threat first can be a fatal mistake if an urgent corner threat expires!

Eliminates the most urgent threat first (Left, 350ms deadline), then sweeps smoothly through Center and Right with positive margins on every target.

Tunnel vision trap. Even though Center was right in front of you, Left was the imminent threat (350ms). By shooting Center first, Left shoots you at 350ms before your turn finishes.

This is why we slice the pie: (Make sure you save a slice for your friend!)

Slicing the pie isn't just tactical dogma—it is the physical movement that enforces optimal permutation scheduling. When you slice a corner incrementally, geometry forces threats to un-occlude in monotonic sweep order (Left → Center → Right). Slicing the pie prevents you from ever triggering the Center-First Reflex Trap or getting caught in a 115° cross-room panic thrash.

In classical single-machine scheduling with sequence-dependent setup times (1 | sjk | Lmax), the optimal sequence π* must balance two conflicting forces:

  • Earliest Deadline First (EDF): High-urgency threats with tight deadlines (such as Left at 350 ms) must be cleared before low-urgency threats (such as Center at 800 ms). Shooting Center first creates a +173 ms deadline breach on Left despite zero initial slew.
  • Shortest Slew Path: Minimizing reticle travel between subsequent threats prevents travel exhaustion from causing a late deadline breach on the final targets.
