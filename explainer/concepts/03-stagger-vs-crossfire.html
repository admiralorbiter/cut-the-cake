03 · REVEAL TIMING & EXPOSURE WORKLOAD

Why can two enemies be worse than three?

Because danger is not determined by raw threat count. It is determined by concurrent workload—how much urgent work arrives at your crosshair at the exact same millisecond.

✗ 2 ENEMIES · SIMULTANEOUS CROSSFIRE
E1 Window:
r₁ = 0 ms (Deadline: 350 ms)
E2 Window:
r₂ = 0 ms (FATAL QUEUE COLLISION)
⚠️ UNFAIR CROSSFIRE (FATAL BOTTLENECK)

Both threats appear simultaneously at opposing 110° angles. Even with instant reaction on E1, turning to E2 takes 170ms, causing E2 to eliminate you before your reticle arrives.

✓ 3 ENEMIES · 1-BY-1 ISOLATION BEHIND COVER
E1 Window:
r₁ = 0 ms (Fight 1)
E2 Window:
r₂ = 450 ms (Fight 2)
E3 Window:
r₃ = 900 ms (Fight 3)
✓ 100% CLEARABLE (ZERO QUEUE OVERLAP)

More total enemies, but zero concurrent overlap. The player eliminates E1, takes a step behind cover to isolate E2, and finishes E3 with comfortable tactical margins on every shot.

Threat count is not workload. Concurrent exposure is workload.
📐 Mathematical Definition: Job Release Windows and Single-Server Queuing
Job Jj Release Time: rj = Time Threat j Becomes Visible in Line-of-Sight

In scheduling theory, the player's aim reticle is modeled as a single server that can only process one threat job at a time:

  • Simultaneous Releases (r₁ = r₂): Two jobs enter the service queue at the exact same millisecond. The server must process J₁ over time A + p, then perform setup transition q = Δθ / ω, then process J₂. If r₁ + A + p + q + p > D₂, the schedule is mathematically infeasible regardless of player skill.
  • Geometry-Driven Stagger (r₂ ≥ r₁ + A + p + q): Geometry buffers the second release, resetting the queue to 0 before J₂ arrives. Even with 10 sequential threats, workload remains 1 at all times.
